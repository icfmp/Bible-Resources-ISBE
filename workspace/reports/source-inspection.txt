<entryFree n="AARON"> <p>ar'-un, sometimes pronounced ar'on ('aharon--Septuagint Aaron, meaning uncertain: Gesenius suggests "mountaineer"; Furst, "enlightened"; others give "rich," "fluent." Cheyne mentions Redslob's "ingenious conjecture" of ha-'aron--"the ark"--with its mythical, priestly significance, Encyclopedia Biblica under the word):</p><p><hi rend="underline">1. Family:</hi></p><p>Probably eldest son of Amram (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.6.20">Ex 6:20</ref>), and according to the uniform genealogical lists (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.6.16-Exod.6.20">Ex 6:16-20</ref>; <ref osisRef="Bible:1Chr.6.1-1Chr.6.3">1Ch 6:1-3</ref>), the fourth from Levi. This however is not certainly fixed, since there are frequent omissions from the Hebrew lists of names which are not prominent in the line of descent. For the corresponding period from Levi to Aaron the Judah list has six names (<ref osisRef="Bible:Ruth.4.18-Ruth.4.20">Ru 4:18-20</ref>; <ref osisRef="Bible:1Chr.2.1-1Chr.2.55">1Ch 2</ref>). Levi and his family were zealous, even to violence (<ref osisRef="Bible:Gen.34.25">Ge 34:25</ref>; <ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.32.26">Ex 32:26</ref>), for the national honor and religion, and Aaron no doubt inherited his full portion of this spirit. His mother's name was Jochebed, who was also of the Levitical family (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.6.20">Ex 6:20</ref>). Miriam, his sister, was several years older, since she was set to watch the novel cradle of the infant brother Moses, at whose birth Aaron was three years old (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.7.7">Ex 7:7</ref>).</p><p><hi rend="underline">2. Becomes Moses' Assistant:</hi></p><p>When Moses fled from Egypt, Aaron remained to share the hardships of his people, and possibly to render them some service; for we are told that Moses entreated of God his brother's cooperation in his mission to Pharaoh and to Israel, and that Aaron went out to meet his returning brother, as the time of deliverance drew near (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.4.27">Ex 4:27</ref>). While Moses, whose great gifts lay along other lines, was slow of speech (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.4.10">Ex 4:10</ref>), Aaron was a ready spokesman, and became his brother's representative, being called his "mouth" (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.4.16">Ex 4:16</ref>) and his "prophet" (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.7.1">Ex 7:1</ref>). After their meeting in the wilderness the two brothers returned together to Egypt on the hazardous mission to which Yahweh had called them (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.4.27-Exod.4.31">Ex 4:27-31</ref>). At first they appealed to their own nation, recalling the ancient promises and declaring the imminent deliverance, Aaron being the spokesman. But the heart of the people, hopeless by reason of the hard bondage and heavy with the care of material things, did not incline to them. The two brothers then forced the issue by appealing directly to Pharaoh himself, Aaron still speaking for his brother (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.6.10-Exod.6.13">Ex 6:10-13</ref>). He also performed, at Moses' direction, the miracles which confounded Pharaoh and his magicians. With Hur, he held up Moses hands, in order that the `rod of God might be lifted up,' during the fight with Amalek (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.17.10">Ex 17:10</ref>,<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.17.12">12</ref>).</p><p><hi rend="underline">3. An Elder:</hi></p><p>Aaron next comes into prominence when at Sinai he is one of the elders and representatives of his tribe to approach nearer to the Mount than the people in general were allowed to do, and to see the manifested glory of God (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.24.1">Ex 24:1</ref>,<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.24.9">9</ref>,<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.24.10">10</ref>). A few days later, when Moses, attended by his "minister" Joshua, went up into the mountain, Aaron exercised some kind of headship over the people in his absence. Despairing of seeing again their leader, who had disappeared into the mystery of communion with the invisible God, they appealed to Aaron to prepare them more tangible gods, and to lead them back to Egypt (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.32.1-Exod.32.35">Ex 32</ref>). Aaron never appears as the strong, heroic character which his brother was; and here at Sinai he revealed his weaker nature, yielding to the demands of the people and permitting the making of the golden bullock. That he must however have yielded reluctantly, is evident from the ready zeal of his tribesmen, whose leader he was, to stay and to avenge the apostasy by rushing to arms and falling mightily upon the idolaters at the call of Moses (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.32.26-Exod.32.28">Ex 32:26-28</ref>).</p><p><hi rend="underline">4. High Priest:</hi></p><p>In connection with the planning and erection of the tabernacle ("the Tent"), Aaron and his sons being chosen for the official priesthood, elaborate and symbolical vestments were prepared for them (<ref osisRef="Bible:Exod.28.1-Exod.28.43">Ex 28</ref>); and after the erection and dedication of the tabernacle, he and his sons were formally inducted into the sacred office (<ref osisRef="Bible:Lev.8.1-Lev.8.36">Le 8</ref>). It appears that Aaron alone was anointed with the holy oil (<ref osisRef="Bible:Lev.8.12">Le 8:12</ref>), but his sons were included with him in the duty of caring for sacrificial rites and things. They served in receiving and presenting the various offerings, and could enter and serve in the first chamber of the tabernacle; but Aaron alone, the high priest, the Mediator of the Old Covenant, could enter into the Holy of Holies, and that only once a year, on the great Day of Atonement (<ref osisRef="Bible:Lev.16.12-Lev.16.14">Le 16:12-14</ref>).</p><p><hi rend="underline">5. Rebels Against Moses:</hi></p><p>After the departure of Israel from Sinai, Aaron joined his sister Miriam in a protest against the authority of Moses (<ref osisRef="Bible:Num.12.1-Num.12.16">Nu 12</ref>), which they asserted to be self-assumed. For this rebellion Miriam was smitten with leprosy, but was made whole again, when, at the pleading of Aaron, Moses interceded with God for her.

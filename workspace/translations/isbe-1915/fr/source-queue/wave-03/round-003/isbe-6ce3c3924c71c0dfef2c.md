# CONFECTION; CONFECTIONARY
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
kon-fek'-shun, kon-fek'-shun-a-ri (roqach "perfume," "spice," raqqahah, feminine "perfumer"):
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) "Confection" is found in the King James Version only and but once "a confection after the art of the apothecary" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ex 30:35⟧; the Revised Version (British and American) "perfume"); but the Revised Version (British and American) renders ⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:1Ch 9:30⟧, "the confection (the King James Version "ointment") of the spices." It stands for something "made up," a mixture of perfumes or medicines, but never sweetmeats, as confection means with us.
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:6 -->
(2) Likewise a "confectionary" is a perfumer. This word, too, is found but once (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:1Sa 8:13⟧), "He will take your daughters to be perfumers (the King James Version "confectionaries"), and to be cooks, and to be bakers."
<!-- ISBE-END:6 -->

# COUCH

Couch (verb): rabhats, "to crouch," "lurk," as a beast in readiness to spring on its prey. "If thou doest not well, sin coucheth at the door" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Ge 4:7⟧, the King James Version "lieth"), waiting for it to open. Cain is warned to beware of the first temptations to evil, in his case especially a sullen and jealous disposition (compare Dante, Inferno, I, 30). See ⟦ISBE:OPEN:7⟧ABEL⟦ISBE:CLOSE:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:OPEN:8⟧CAIN⟦ISBE:CLOSE:8⟧. The tribe of Judah is compared for its bravery to a recumbent lion or lioness (⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ge 49:9⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Nu 24:9⟧ f); and Issachar to "a strong ass, couching down between the sheepfolds" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Ge 49:14⟧, the King James Version "between two burdens"; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Jud 5:16⟧). "The deep that coucheth beneath" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:De 33:13⟧), probably the springs of water, or possibly, as Driver suggests, "the subterranean deep, pictured as a gigantic monster."

# DESOLATE
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
des'-o-lat (very frequently in the Old Testament for shamem, and its derivatives; less frequently, charebh, and its derivatives, and other words. In the New Testament it stands for eremos (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Mt 23:38⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ac 1:20⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Ga 4:27⟧) eremoo (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Re 17:16⟧), and monoo (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:1Ti 5:5⟧)): From Latin de, intens., solus, alone. Several shades of meaning can be distinguished:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:8 -->
(1) Its primary sense is "left lonely," "forlorn," e. g. ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ps 25:16⟧, "Have mercy upon me; for I am desolate" (Hebrew yachidh, "alone"); ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:1Ti 5:5⟧, "she that is a widow indeed, and desolate" (Greek memonomene, "left alone").
<!-- ISBE-END:8 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:11 -->
(2) In the sense of "laid waste," "destitute of inhabitants," e. g. ⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Jer 4:7⟧, "to make thy land desolate, that thy cities be laid waste, without inhabitant."
<!-- ISBE-END:11 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:13 -->
(3) With the meaning "comfortless," "afflicted," e. g. ⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ps 143:4⟧, "My heart within me is desolate."
<!-- ISBE-END:13 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:15 -->
(4) In the sense of "barren," "childless," "unfruitful," e. g. ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Job 15:34⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Isa 49:21⟧ (Hebrew galmudh).
<!-- ISBE-END:15 -->

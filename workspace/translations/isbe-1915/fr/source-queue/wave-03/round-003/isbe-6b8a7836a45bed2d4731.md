# CLEAVE
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
klev: Is used in the Bible in two different senses:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) baqa` "to split," or "to rend." We are told that Abraham "clave the wood for the burnt-offering" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ge 22:3⟧), and that "they clave the wood of the cart" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:1Sa 6:14⟧). The Psalmist speaks of Yahweh cleaving fountain and flood (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Ps 74:15⟧), and the plowman cleaving the earth (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Ps 141:7⟧). For other examples see ⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Jud 15:19⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ec 10:9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Ps 78:15⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Hab 3:9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:12 -->
(2) dabhaq; kollao, "to adhere to," or "to join one's self to." This meaning is the reverse of the preceding. The Psalmist speaks of his tongue cleaving to the roof of his mouth (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Ps 137:6⟧). We are told that a man should cleave unto his wife (⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ge 2:24⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Mt 19:5⟧). It is said that Ru clave unto her mother-in-law (⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Ru 1:14⟧), and that certain men clave unto Paul (⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Ac 17:34⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:Ac 4:23⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:18:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:11:23⟧ margin).
<!-- ISBE-END:12 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:20 -->
"Cleave" is also used in this sense to describe one's adherence to principles. Paul admonished the Romans to cleave to that which is good (⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Ro 12:9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:21:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:20 -->

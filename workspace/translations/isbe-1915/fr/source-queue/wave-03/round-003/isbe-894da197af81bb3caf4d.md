# CUMBER; CUMBERED
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
kum'-ber, (katargeo, "to make idle," perispaomai, "to be drawn about," in mind "to be distracted"): Spoken of the barren fig tree in the parable: "Cut it down; why doth it also cumber (block up, make unproductive) the ground?" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Lu 13:7⟧). Cumbered means to be over-occupied with cares or business, distracted: "But Martha was cumbered about much serving" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Lu 10:40⟧). The word cumbrance occurs only in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:De 1:12⟧: "How can I myself alone bear your cumbrance?" (Torach, "an encumbrance," "a burden"). Compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Isa 1:14⟧, where the Revised Version, margin has "cumbrance," the Revised Version (British and American) "trouble."

# DARIC
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
dar'-ik (darkemon, and 'adharkon; dareikos): A Persian gold coin about a guinea or five dollars in value. The first form of the word occurs in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:1Ch 29:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ezr 2:69⟧, and ⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Ne 7:70-72⟧; the second in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Ezr 8:27⟧ and is rendered, "dram" in the King James Version and "daric" in the Revised Version (British and American). In the passage in Chronicles, it must refer to a weight, since at the time of David there were no coins, but in the days of Ezra and Nehemiah the Persian darics were current. See ⟦ISBE:OPEN:7⟧MONEY⟦ISBE:CLOSE:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧

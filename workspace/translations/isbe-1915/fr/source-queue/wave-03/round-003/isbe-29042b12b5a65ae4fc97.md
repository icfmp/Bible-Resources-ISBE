# DISCERN
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
di-zurn': Five Hebrew words are thus translated: bin, yadha`, nakhar, ra'ah and shama`. It may simply mean "observe" (bin), "I discerned among the youths" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Pr 7:7⟧); or discriminating knowlege, "A wise man's heart discerneth time and judgment" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ec 8:5⟧, yadha`); "He discerned him not, because his hands," etc. (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Ge 27:23⟧, nakhar); "Then shall ye return and discern between the righteous and the wicked" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Mal 3:18⟧, ra'ah); "So is my lord the king to discern good," etc. (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:2Sa 14:17⟧, shama`). In the New Testament the words anakrino, diakrino and dokimazo are thus translated, expressing close and distinct acquaintance with or a critical knowledge of things. Used in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:1Co 2:14⟧ the King James Version of "the things of the spirit of God"; in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:1Co 11:29⟧ of "the (Lord's) body" in the sacrament; in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Mt 16:3⟧ of "the face of the heaven"; in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Heb 5:14⟧ of a clear knowledge of good and evil as the prerogative of a full-grown man.

# CRAFT; CRAFTINESS; CRAFTY
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
kraft, kraf'-ti-nes, kraf'-ti, (panourgia), (panourgos): The original meaning is that of "ability to do anything," universally applied in a bad sense to unscrupulous wickedness, that stops short of no measure, however reprehensible, in order to attain its purposes; then, in a modified form, to resourcefulness in wrong, cunning (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Da 8:25⟧; 2 Macc 12:24; the Revised Version, margin "jugglery"). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Lu 20:23⟧, Jesus perceives "the craftiness" of His adversaries, i.e. the complicated network which they have laid to ensnare Him. The art with which a plot is concealed, and its direction to the ruin of others, are elements that enter into the meaning. Heinrici on ⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:1Co 3:19⟧ illustrates from Plato the distinction between craftiness and wisdom. There is a touch of humor in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:2Co 12:16⟧, when Paul speaks of his conduct toward the Corinthians as having been "crafty."

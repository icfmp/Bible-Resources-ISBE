# ELDER IN THE NEW TESTAMENT
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
(presbuteros):
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) The word is used adjectivally to denote seniority (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Lu 15:25⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:1Ti 5:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:6 -->
(2) Referring to the Jewish elders of the synagogue, usually associated with the scribes and Pharisees, and New Testament passages cited in the previous article.
<!-- ISBE-END:6 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:7 -->
(3) It denotes certain persons appointed to hold office in the Christian church, and to exercise spiritual oversight over the flock entrusted to them. From the references in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ac 14:23⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:20:17⟧ it may be inferred that the churches generally had elders appointed over them. That "elders" and "bishops" were in apostolic and sub-apostolic times the same, is now almost universally admitted; in all New Testament references their functions are identical. The most probable explanation of the difference of names is that "elder" refers mainly to the person, and "bishop" to the office; the name "elder" emphasizes what he is, while "bishop," that is "overseer," emphasizes what the elder or presbyter does.

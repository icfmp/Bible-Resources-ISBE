# DALE, KING'S
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
(dal, `emeq hamelekh) :
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) "Absalom in his lifetime had taken and reared up for himself the pillar, which is in the king's dale" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:2Sa 18:18⟧). According to Josephus (Ant., VII, x, 3) this was a marble pillar, which he calls "Absalom's hand" and it was two furlongs from Jerusalem. Warren suggests that this dale was identical with the ⟦ISBE:OPEN:5⟧KING'S GARDEN⟦ISBE:CLOSE:5⟧ (which see), which he places at the open valley formed at the junction of the Tyropoen with the Kidron (see ⟦ISBE:OPEN:6⟧JERUSALEM⟦ISBE:CLOSE:6⟧). The so-called Absalom's Pillar, which the Jews still pelt with stones in reprobation of Absalom's disobedience, and which a comparatively recent tradition associates with ⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:2Sa 18:18⟧, is a very much later structure, belonging to the Greco- Roman period, but showing Egyptian influence.

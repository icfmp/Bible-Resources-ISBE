# DROP, DROPPING
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
"To drop" expresses a "distilling" or "dripping" of a fluid (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Jud 5:4⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Pr 3:20⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:So 5:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:13⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Joe 3:18⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Am 9:13⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:1Sa 14:26⟧, "the honey dropped" (margin "a stream of honey")); ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Job 29:22⟧ and ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Isa 45:8⟧ read "distil" (the King James Version "drop"). The continuous "droppings" of rain through a leaking roof (roofs were usually made of clay in Palestine, and always liable to cracks and leakage) on a "very rainy day" is compared to a contentious wife (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Pr 19:13⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:12:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:27:15⟧); "What is described is the irritating, unceasing, sound of the fall, drop after drop, of water through the chinks in the roof" (Plumptre, in the place cited); compare also the King James Version ⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ec 10:18⟧ (the Revised Version (British and American) "leaketh").

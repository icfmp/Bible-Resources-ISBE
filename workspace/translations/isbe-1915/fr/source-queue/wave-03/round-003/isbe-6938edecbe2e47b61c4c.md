# CHOKE
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
chok (pnigo, and its compounds): Is used in its primary sense of "to strangle," or "to suffocate," in describing the fate of the swine (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Lu 8:33⟧ the King James Version). The Revised Version (British and American) has "drowned," but "choked" is the correct rendering of the Greek word.
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:4 -->
Figurative: It is used in the sense of "to strangle" "smother," "suffocate," as if by depriving of breath, in describing the fate of the young grain growing in the midst of thorns (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Mt 13:7⟧). The figurative is carried a little farther still in describing the way the word, planted in the heart, is overcome by the care of the world, and the deceitfulness of riches (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Mt 13:22⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧

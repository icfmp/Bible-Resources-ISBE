# CONFOUND
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
kon-found': The physical origin of spiritual terms is well illustrated by the principal Hebrew words for "confounded" (rendered also "ashamed," etc.); bosh, is "to become pale" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:2Ki 19:26⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Job 6:20⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Ps 83:17⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:129:5⟧ the King James Version; Isa 19:9, etc.); chapher, "to become red" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Ps 35:4⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Isa 1:29⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:24:23⟧, "the moon shall be confounded," Mic 3:7); yabhash, "to be dried up" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Jer 46:24⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Jer 48:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:20⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Jer 50:2⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Zec 10:5⟧); kalam, "to blush" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Ps 69:6⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Isa 41:11⟧, etc.). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Ge 11:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:17:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:9⟧, of the confusion of tongues, the word is balal, "to mix," "mingle." In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Jer 1:17⟧ the King James Version it is chathath, "to bring or put down."
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

In New Testament, kataischuno, "to put to shame" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:1Co 1:27⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:1Pe 2:6⟧ the King James Version); and sugchuno, "to pour together," "bewilder' " (⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:Ac 2:6⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:23:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:24:9:22⟧). the Revised Version (British and American) frequently gives "ashamed" and "put to shame" instead of "confounded."

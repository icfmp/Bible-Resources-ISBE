# DAUGHTER
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
do'-ter (bath; thugater): Used in Scriptures in several more or less distinct senses:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(a) for daughter in the ordinary, literal sense (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ge 46:25⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Ex 1:16⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:6 -->
(b) daughter-in-law (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Ru 2:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:6 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:8 -->
(c) grand-daughter or other female descendant (⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ex 21⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Lu 1:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:13:16⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:8 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:12 -->
(d) the women of a country, or of a place, taken collectively (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Lu 23:28⟧), of a particular religion (⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Mal 2:11⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:12 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:15 -->
(e) all the population of a place, taken collectively, especially in Prophets and poetic books (⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Ps 9:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Isa 23:10⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:17:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:Jer 46:24⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:18:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Mt 21:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:19:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:15 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:20 -->
(f) used in familiar address, "Daughter, be of good comfort" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Mt 9:22⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:Mr 5:34⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:22:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:Lu 8:48⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:23:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:20 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:24 -->
(g) women in general (⟦ISBE:KEEP:25:Pr 31:29⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:25:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:24 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:26 -->
(h) the personification of towns or cities, as of the female sex (⟦ISBE:KEEP:27:Isa 47:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:27:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:28:Eze 16:44⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:28:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:29:46⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:30:Na 3:4⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:30:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:31:7⟧), especially of dependent towns and villages (⟦ISBE:KEEP:32:Ps 48:11⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:32:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:33:Nu 21:25⟧ margin; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:34:Jud 1:27⟧ margin);
<!-- ISBE-END:26 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:35 -->
(i) in Hebrew idiom for person or thing belonging to or having the characteristics of that with which it is joined, as "daughter of ninety years," of Sarah, ninety years old (⟦ISBE:KEEP:36:Ge 17:17⟧); "daughters of music," singing birds, or singing women (⟦ISBE:KEEP:37:Ec 12:4⟧); daughters of a tree, i. e. branches; daughter of the eye, i. e. the pupil.
<!-- ISBE-END:35 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:38 -->
Daughters were not so highly prized as sons, not being usually mentioned by name. A father might sometimes sell his daughter as bondwoman (⟦ISBE:KEEP:39:Ex 21:7⟧); though not to a foreigner (⟦ISBE:KEEP:40:Ex 21:8⟧); daughters might sometimes inherit as did sons, but could not take the inheritance outside of the tribe (⟦ISBE:KEEP:41:Nu 36:1-12⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:41:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:38 -->

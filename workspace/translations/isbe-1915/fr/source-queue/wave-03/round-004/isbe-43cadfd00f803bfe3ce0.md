# DOUBT
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
dout: This word, found only a score of times in the Bible, translates nevertheless about half as many different Hebrew and Greek originals with a corresponding variety of meanings.
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ge 37:33⟧ "without doubt" is to be taken in the common sense of "certainly"; in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Job 12:2⟧ in the sarcastic sense of "indeed!" In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Da 5:12⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:16⟧, it is used as a difficult problem or mystery to be explained, and these are the only cases of its employment in the Old Testament.
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:8 -->
In the New Testament it is about equally used to translate diaporeo, and diakrino, and their cognates. The first means "to be without resource," "utterly at a loss," "nonplussed"; and the second, "to judge diversely." For the first, see ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Joh 13:22⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Ac 2:12⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Ac 5:24⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Ac 10:17⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Ac 25:20⟧ the King James Version; and ⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ga 4:20⟧ the King James Version. For the second see ⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Mt 21:21⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:15:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Mr 11:23⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Ac 10:20⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:17:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:Ro 14:23⟧. The last-named is deserving of particular attention. "He that doubteth is condemned (the King James Version "damned") if he eat," means that in a case of uncertainty as to one's Christian liberty, it were better to err on the side of restraint. In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Lu 12:29⟧ "to be of doubtful mind" (meteorizo, literally, "to suspend"; see Thayer, under the word), means "to be driven by gusts," or "to fluctuate in mid-air."
<!-- ISBE-END:8 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:20 -->
Here, as in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Mt 14:31⟧, "doubt" does not indicate a lack of faith, but rather "a state of qualified faith": its weakness, but not its absence.
<!-- ISBE-END:20 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:22 -->
In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:Joh 10:24⟧ "doubt" translates airo psuchen, which literally means "to lift up the soul" or "to keep one in suspense"; so the Revised Version (British and American). See also ⟦ISBE:OPEN:24⟧DISPUTATION⟦ISBE:CLOSE:24⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:24:tail⟧

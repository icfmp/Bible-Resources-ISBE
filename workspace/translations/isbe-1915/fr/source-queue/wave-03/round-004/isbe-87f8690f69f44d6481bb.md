# DEPOSIT
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
de-poz'-it (paratheke, ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:1Ti 6:20⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:2Ti 1:12⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:14⟧ the Revised Version, margin, paraphrased in both the King James Version and the Revised Version (British and American) into "that which is committed" (see ⟦ISBE:OPEN:6⟧COMMEND⟦ISBE:CLOSE:6⟧)): The noun was used in the classical Greek, just as its English equivalents, for "that which is placed with another for safe keeping," a charge committed to another's hands, consisting often of money or property; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Ex 22:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Le 6:2⟧. This practice was common in days when there were no banks.
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:9 -->
(1) In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:1Ti 6:20⟧; also ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:2Ti 1:14⟧, the reference is to a deposit which God makes with man, and for which man is to give a reckoning. The context shows that this deposit is the Christian faith, "the pattern of sound words" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:2Ti 1:13⟧), that which is contrasted with the "oppositions of the knowledge which is falsely so called" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:1Ti 6:20⟧). "Keep the talent of the Christian faith safe and undiminished" (Vincentius Lirenensis).
<!-- ISBE-END:9 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:14 -->
(2) In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:2Ti 1:12⟧, the deposit is one which man makes with God. The key to the meaning of this expression is found probably in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Ps 31:5⟧: "Into thy hand I commend my spirit: Thou hast redeemed me," i.e. "All that I am, with all my interests, have been entrusted to Thy safe keeping, and, therefore, I have no anxieties with respect to the future. The day of reckoning, `that day,' will show how faithful are the hands that hold this trust."
<!-- ISBE-END:14 -->

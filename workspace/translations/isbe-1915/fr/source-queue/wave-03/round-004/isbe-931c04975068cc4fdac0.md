# DOOR
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
dor: Most commonly the rendering of Hebrew pethach, "doorway," deleth, "door" proper (the two distinguished in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ge 19:6⟧), or of Greek thura, which represents both meanings. The door proper was usually of wood, frequently sheeted with metal, sometimes of one slab of stone, as shown in excavations in the Hauran. It turned on pivots (the "hinges" of ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Pr 26:14⟧) working in sockets above and below, and was provided with a bolt (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:2Sa 13:17⟧) or with lock and key (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Jud 3:23⟧). The doorway was enclosed by the stone threshold (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:1Ki 14:17⟧), the two doorposts on either side, and the lintel above (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ex 12:7⟧). Doors were frequently two-leaved, and folding ones are mentioned in connection with the temple (⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:1Ki 6:34⟧). Where "door" is used in connectio with city gates (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Ne 3:1⟧ ff) it refers to the door proper which swings on its hinges as distinguished from the whole structure. The custom of fastening to the doorposts small cases containing a parchment inscribed with the words of ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:De 6:4-9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:11:13-21⟧ had its origin in the command there given.

# CUT; CUTTING

(karath, gadha`, kachadh, nathach; apokopto, ekkopto): Many Hebrew words are translated "cut." Of these karath, "to cut down, out, off," is the most frequent. As "cut off" it is used in the sense of laying or destroying (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ge 9:11⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:De 12:29⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:1Ki 11:16⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Ps 101:8⟧, etc.), also for cutting off transgressors from the community of Yahweh, which meant probably separation, or exclusion, rather than death or destruction (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Ge 17:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ex 12:15⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:19⟧). Other words are damam, ("to be silent," "cease" ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Jer 25:37⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Jer 48:2⟧); tsamath "to destroy" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Ps 54:5⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Ps 94:23⟧, etc.); gadhadh, "to cut, one's self," is used of the cutting of one's flesh before heathen gods and in mourning for the dead, which was forbidden to the Israelites, (⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:De 14:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:1Ki 18:28⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:15:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Jer 16:6⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:41:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:17:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:47:5⟧); sereT, sareTeth, "incision," are also used of those "cuttings of the flesh" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Le 19:28⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:20:Le 21:5⟧).

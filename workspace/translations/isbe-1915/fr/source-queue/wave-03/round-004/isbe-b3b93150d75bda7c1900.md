In the ideal social system of the Old Testament, debts are incurred only because of poverty, and the law protected the poor debtor from his creditor, who in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Ex 22:25⟧ is forbidden to demand interest, and in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:De 15:2⟧ to exact payment in view of the nearness of the year of release. ⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:2Ki 4:1⟧ shows that the actual practice was not so considerate, and in consequence the creditor fell into bad repute. In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Ps 109:11⟧ he is the extortioner; in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:20:Pr 29:13⟧ the oppressor is evidently the creditor, though a different word is used; compare also ⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Pr 22:7⟧. In Sirach 29:28 the importunity of the creditor is one of the hardships of the poor man of understanding. The actual practice of the Jews may be gathered from ⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:Ne 5:1⟧ ff; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:Jer 34:8⟧ ff; and Sirach 29:1-11.

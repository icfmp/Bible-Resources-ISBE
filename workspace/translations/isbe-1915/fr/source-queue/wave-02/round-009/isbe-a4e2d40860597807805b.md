# CHEER; CHEERFULNESS
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
cher, cher'-fool-nes: The English word "cheer" meant:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) originally face, countenance (Greek kara, "head," through Old French, chere, "face"),
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:4 -->
(2) then the expression on the face, especially
<!-- ISBE-END:4 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:5 -->
(3) the expression of good spirits, and finally
<!-- ISBE-END:5 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:6 -->
(4) good spirits, without any reference to the facial expression.
<!-- ISBE-END:6 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:7 -->
The noun "cheer" in English Versions of the Bible is only found with adjective "good" (except 1 Esdras 9:54, "great cheer"), the word not having quite lost its earlier neutral character (any face expression, whether joyous or otherwise). In Old Testament, Tobh, is translated "cheer," "let thy heart cheer thee" (see ⟦ISBE:OPEN:8⟧GOOD⟦ISBE:CLOSE:8⟧); sameach, "to rejoice" is so translated in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:De 24:5⟧, "shall cheer his wife" (the King James Version "cheer up his wife"), and ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Jud 9:13⟧, "wine, which cheereth God ('elohim) and man."

# CHAIN; CHAINS
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
chan, chanz: Chains were used by the Hebrews:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) As ornaments: 'ets`adhah, neTiphah, `anaq, rabhidh, sharsherah, rattoq. As ornaments for the person they were worn about the ankles (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Nu 31:50⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Isa 3:20⟧) and about the neck (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:So 4:9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Eze 16:11⟧). They were used as ornaments for the ephod and breastplate of the high priest (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ex 28:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:39:15⟧). These chains were of pure gold. Solomon placed chains before the oracle in the temple (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:1Ki 6:21⟧), and these were also of pure gold. They were used as ornaments for graven images (⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Isa 40:19⟧) and around the necks of prized animals. This was true of the camels taken from the Midianites by Gideon (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Jud 8:21⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:12:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:26⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:13:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:14 -->
(2) As marks of distinction: rabhidh, hamunekh (hamnikh): That seems to be true of the chain which Pharaoh placed about the neck of Joseph (⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Ge 41:42⟧), and of the one which the king of Babylon promised to the wise men (⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Da 5:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:14 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:17 -->
(3) As means of confining prisoners: nechosheth; halusis: A number of passages that were translated "chains" in the King James Version are translated "fetters" in the Revised Version (British and American) (see ⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:Jud 16:21⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:18:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:2Sa 3:34⟧). Among the Romans the prisoner was chained to one or two guards (⟦ISBE:KEEP:20:Ac 12:6⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:20:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:21:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:21:33⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:22:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:Eph 6:20⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:23:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:24:2Ti 1:16⟧). These chains were perhaps made of copper or an alloy of copper and tin.
<!-- ISBE-END:17 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:25 -->
(4) As a figurative expression: `anaq. The Psalmist likens pride to a chain about the neck (⟦ISBE:KEEP:26:Ps 73:6⟧), and in Pr it is stated that the young man who hears the instruction of his father and forsakes not the law of his mother shall find that they are chains about his neck (⟦ISBE:KEEP:27:Pr 1:9⟧). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:28:Re 20:1⟧ the angel is described as descending with a great chain in his hand. According to the King James Version Peter speaks of the fallen angels as having been delivered into "chains of darkness" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:29:2Pe 2:4⟧), seira, and Jude speaks of them as being reserved in "everlasting chains" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:30:Jude 1:6⟧, the Revised Version (British and American) "bonds"), desmos.
<!-- ISBE-END:25 -->

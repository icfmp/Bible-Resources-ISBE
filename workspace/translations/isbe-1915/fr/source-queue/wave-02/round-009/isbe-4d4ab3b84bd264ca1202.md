# BETHANY
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
beth'-a-ni (Bethania):
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) A village, 15 furlongs from Jerusalem (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Joh 11:18⟧), on the road to Jericho, at the Mount of Olives (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Mr 11:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Lu 19:29⟧), where lived "Simon the leper" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Mr 14:3⟧) and Mary, Martha and Lazarus (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Joh 11:18⟧ f). This village may justifiably be called the Judean home of Jesus, as He appears to have preferred to lodge there rather than in Jerusalem itself (⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Mt 21:17⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Mr 11:11⟧). Here occurred the incident of the raising of Lazarus (⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Joh 11⟧) and the feast at the house of Simon (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Mt 26:1-13⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:12:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Mr 14:3-9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:13:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Lu 7:36-50⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Joh 1:2:1-8⟧). The Ascension as recorded in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Lu 24:50-51⟧ is thus described: "He led them out until they were over against Bethany: and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them. And it came to pass, while he blessed them, he parted from them, and was carried up into heaven."
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:17 -->
Bethany is today el `Azareyeh ("the place of Lazarus"--the L being displaced to form the article). It is a miserably untidy and tumble-down village facing East on the Southeast slope of the Mount of Olives, upon the carriage road to Jericho. A fair number of fig, almond and olive trees surround the houses. The traditional tomb of Lazarus is shown and there are some remains of medieval buildings, besides rock-cut tombs of much earlier date (PEF, III, 27, Sheet XVII).
<!-- ISBE-END:17 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:18 -->
(2) "Bethany beyond the Jordan" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Joh 1:28⟧; the King James Version Bethabara; Bethabara, a reading against the majority of the manuscripts, supported by Origen on geographical grounds): No such place is known. Grove suggested that the place intended is ⟦ISBE:OPEN:20⟧BETH-NIMRAH⟦ISBE:CLOSE:20⟧ (which see), the modern Tell nimrin, a singularly suitable place, but hard to fit in with ⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Joh 1:28⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:Joh 2:1⟧. The traditional site is the ford East of Jericho.

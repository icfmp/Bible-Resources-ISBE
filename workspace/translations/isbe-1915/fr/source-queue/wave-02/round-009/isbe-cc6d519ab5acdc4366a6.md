# BATH-SHEBA
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
bath-she'-ba, bath'-she-ba (bath-shebha`, "the seventh daughter," or "the daughter of an oath," also called Bathshua bath- shua`, "the daughter of opulence" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:1Ch 3:5⟧); the Septuagint however reads Bersabee everywhere; compare ⟦ISBE:OPEN:4⟧BATHSHUA⟦ISBE:CLOSE:4⟧; HPN, 65, 67, 77, 206 for Bath-sheba, and 67, 69, note 3, for Bathshua): Bath-sheba was the daughter of Eliam (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:2Sa 11:3⟧) or Ammiel (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:1Ch 3:5⟧); both names have the same meaning. She was the beautiful wife of Uriah the Hittite, and because of her beauty was forced by David to commit adultery (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:2Sa 11:2 ff;⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ps 51⟧). Her husband Uriah was treacherously killed by the order of David (⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:2Sa 11:6⟧ ff). After the death of her husband David made her his wife and she lived with him in the palace (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:2Sa 11:27⟧). Four sons sprang from this marriage (⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:2Sa 5:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:1Ch 3:5⟧), after the first child, the adulterine, had died (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:2Sa 12:14⟧ ff). With the help of the prophet Nathan she renders futile the usurpation of Adonijah and craftily secures the throne for her son Solomon (⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:1Ki 1:11⟧ ff). Later Adonijah succeeds in deceiving Bath-sheba, but his plan is frustrated by the king (⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:1Ki 2:13⟧ ff). According to Jewish tradition, ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Pr 31⟧ is written by Solomon in memory of his mother. In the genealogy of Jesus (⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Mt 16⟧) Bath-sheba is mentioned as the former wife of Uriah and the mother of Solomon by David.

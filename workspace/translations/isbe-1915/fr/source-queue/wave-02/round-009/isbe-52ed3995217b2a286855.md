# BORROWING
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
bor'-o-ing:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) In the Old Testament period loans were not of a commercial nature, i.e. they were not granted to enable a man to start or run a business. They were really a form of charity, and were made by the lender only to meet the pressure of poverty. To the borrower they were esteemed a form of misfortune (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:De 28:12⟧ f), and by the lender a form of beneficence. Hence, the tone of the Mosaic legislation on the subject.
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:5 -->
(2) Laying interest upon the poor of Israel was forbidden in all the codes (see ⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Ex 22:25⟧ (JE); ⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:De 23:19⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Le 25:36⟧ H), because it was looked upon as making unwarranted profit out of a brother's distress: "If thou lend money to any of my people with thee that is poor, thou shalt not be to him as a creditor; neither shall ye lay upon him interest .... and it shall come to pass, when he crieth unto me, that I will hear; for I am gracious."
<!-- ISBE-END:5 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:9 -->
(3) The Law, however, allowed interest to be taken of a foreigner, or non-Jew (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:De 23:20⟧: "Unto a foreigner thou mayest lend upon interest"; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:De 15:3⟧); and even among Jews pledges were allowed under limitations, or taken against the law (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:De 24:10⟧; compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Job 24:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:13:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:3⟧ "There are that remove the landmarks .... they take the widow's ox for a pledge"). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:De 15:1⟧ ff there is a remarkable law providing a "release" by the creditor every "seven years," a "letting drop of loans" (see Driver in the place cited.). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Ex 3:22⟧, the King James Version "shall borrow" is rendered "shall ask" in the Revised Version (British and American).
<!-- ISBE-END:9 -->

# BORDER; BORDERS
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
bor'-der: Indicating in both singular and plural the outlines or territory of a country. In the sense of "limits," "boundaries" or "territory," it occurs as a translation of gebhul (and its feminine gebhulah, in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ps 74:17⟧) in numerous passages in Old Testament, especially in Josh. yerekhdh = "the flank," "the side," "the coast," hence, "the border" occurs in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ge 49:13⟧; qatseh = "an extremity" "brim," "brink," "edge" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Ex 16:35⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:19:12⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Jos 4:19⟧); micgereth = something enclosing, i.e. "a margin" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ex 37:12⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:1Ki 7:28 f⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:31 f⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:35 f⟧  the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:2Ki 16:17⟧ the King James Version); saphah or sepheth = "the lip" (as a natural boundary) hence, "a margin" "brim," "brink," "edge" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ex 28:26⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:39:19⟧ the King James Version); qets = "an extremity" "end" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:2Ki 19:23⟧ the King James Version); totsa'ah = "exit," hence, "boundary" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:1Ch 5:16⟧); tor = "a succession" "a string" "row," hence, "border" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:So 1:11⟧ the King James Version); yadh = "hand," used in a great variety of applications, both literally and figuratively, proximate and remote; but how it should be translated with "border" in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:1Ch 7:29⟧ is not clear; better would be: "in the hands of the children of Manasseh." Three Greek words occur for the idea: kraspedon = "a margin," "fringe" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:20:Mt 23:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:20:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Mr 6:56⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:21:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:Lu 8:44⟧); horion = "a limit," "a boundary line" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:Mt 4:13⟧); methorios = "contiguous" (neuter plural as noun, "frontier," "border" in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:24:Mr 7:24⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:24:tail⟧

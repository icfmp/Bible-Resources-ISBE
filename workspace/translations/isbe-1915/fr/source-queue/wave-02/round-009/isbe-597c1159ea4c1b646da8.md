# CHANNEL
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
chan'-el ('aphiq (root 'aphaq, "to hold or contain," "to be strong"; compare Arabic 'afaq "to overcome" and 'afiq, "preeminent"); shibboleth (shabhal, "to go," "to go up or grow," "to flow"; compare Arabic 'asbal, "to flow," "to rain," "to put forth ears"; sabalat, "an ear of grain"; sabil, "a road," "a public fountain")): In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Job 12:21⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:40:18⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:41:15⟧ we have 'aphiq in the sense of "strong" (but compare 40:18, the Revised Version (British and American) "tubes" (of brass)). Elsewhere it is translated "river," "brook," "stream," "channel" or "watercourse." Shibboleth (in the dialect of Ephraim cibboleth (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Jud 12:6⟧)) means "an ear of grain" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Ge 41:5⟧ ff; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Ru 2:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Isa 17:5⟧) or "a flood of water" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:Ps 69:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:15⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:Isa 27:12⟧). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:2Sa 22:16⟧ (compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ps 18:15⟧) we have:  "Then the channels of the sea appeared, The foundations of the world were laid bare, By the rebuke of Yahweh, At the blast of the breath of his nostrils."  This is reminiscent of "fountains of the deep" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Ge 7:11⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:15:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:8:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Pr 8:28⟧). It is a question how far we should attribute to these ancient writers a share in modern notions of oceanography, but the idea seems to be that of a withdrawal of the water of the ocean, and the laying bare of submarine declivities and channels such as we know to exist as the result of erosion during a previous period of elevation, when the given portion of ocean floor was dry land.

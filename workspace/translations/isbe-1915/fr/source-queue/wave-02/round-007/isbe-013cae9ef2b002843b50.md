# BONDMAN
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
bond'-man: One of the translations of the word `ebhedh, very common in the Old Testament. It refers to the ordinary slave, either foreign (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ge 43:18⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:44:9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:33⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Le 25:44⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:46⟧) or Hebrew (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Le 25:42⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:2Ki 4:1⟧). Hebrews were forbidden to enslave Hebrews, but did it nevertheless. It also refers to the Israelites in the bondage of Egypt (⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:De 15:15⟧, and often), and in the exile of Babylonia (⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Ezr 9:9⟧). The intended treatment of the men of Judah in Samaria (⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:2Ch 28:10⟧) was apparently to sell them into ordinary slavery or bondage. The word is used once in the New Testament (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Re 6:15⟧) to translate doulos, where it evidently means a slave in contrast with a freeman.

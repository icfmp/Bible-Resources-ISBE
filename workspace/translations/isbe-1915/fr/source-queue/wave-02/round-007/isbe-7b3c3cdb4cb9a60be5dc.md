# BDELLIUM
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
del'-i-um (bedholach): The word occurs twice in the Pentateuch:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Ge 2:12⟧, in conjunction with gold and onyx, as a product of the land of ⟦ISBE:OPEN:5⟧HAVILAH⟦ISBE:CLOSE:5⟧ (which see), and
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:6 -->
(2) in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Nu 11:7⟧, where the manna is likened to this substance in appearance: "The appearance thereof as the appearance of bdellium." The latter comparison excludes the idea of bedholach being a precious stone, and points to the identification of it with the fragrant resinous gum known to the Greeks as bdellion, several kinds being mentioned by Dioscorides and Pliny. It was a product of Arabia, India, Afghanistan, etc.

# BALTASAR
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
bal-ta'-sar (Baltasar; the King James Version Balthasar):
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:3 -->
(1) The Greek of Hebrew, belTesha'tstsar, or belTe'shatstsar, perhaps corresponding to BalaT-sar-ucur, "protect the life of the king," the Babylonian cognomen of Daniel. Compare Belteshazzar (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Da 1:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:2:26⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:4:8⟧ ff, et al.).
<!-- ISBE-END:3 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:7 -->
(2) Baltasar is also the Greek of the Hebrew belsha'tstsar, or bel'shatstsar, the name of the last king of Babylon (corresponding to the Babylonian Bel-sar-ucur; Schrader, Die Keilinschriften und das Alte Testament, III, 396; Syriac Blitshazzar; Vulgate (Jerome's Latin Bible, 390-405 A. D.) Baltassar). Compare Baruch 1 11 and Belshazzar (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Da 5:1 ff;⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:7:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:8:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:7 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:11 -->
(3) The name of one of the Magi who according to the legend visited Jesus at Bethlehem: Melchior from Nubia, Balthasar from Godolia, Caspar from Tharsis.
<!-- ISBE-END:11 -->

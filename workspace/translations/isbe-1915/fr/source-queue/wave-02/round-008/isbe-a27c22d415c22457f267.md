# CAST
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
In general "to throw," with various degrees of violence; usually, with force, but not so necessarily, as e.g. in "cast a net," "cast lots." When applied to molten metal, as in English, first, "to let run. into molds," with reference to their descent by gravity, and, then, "to form," as in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ex 25:12⟧, etc. Usually in the New Testament for ballo, but not always. Thus, in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Lu 1:29⟧ "cast in her mind" means "considered" (dielogizeto); "cast reproach" for Greek oneidizon, "reproached" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Mt 27:44⟧); "casting down" for kathaireo, "demolishing" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:2Co 10:4⟧); "casting all anxiety upon" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:1Pe 5:7⟧), a still stronger term, as in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Lu 17:2⟧ the King James Version; ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ac 27:19⟧. As a fundamental Greek word, it is compounded with many prepositions, "about," "away," "down," "forth," "in," "into," "off," "out," "up," "upon." "Cast down" in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:2Co 4:9⟧ the King James Version is used in a military sense of one prostrated, but not killed in battle. Compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Ps 42:5⟧ with the Revised Version, margin. "Castaway" of the King James Version in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:1Co 9:27⟧, is in the Revised Version (British and American) "rejected" (compare ⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Hebrews 6:8⟧), adokimos, i.e. what the application of a test shows to be counterfeit, or unfit; translated "reprobate" in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:Ro 1:28⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:2Co 13:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:15:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:6⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:7⟧, etc.

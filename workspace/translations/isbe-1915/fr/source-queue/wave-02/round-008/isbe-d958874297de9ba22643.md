# CENSER
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
sen'-ser: In the King James Version censer is used as a translation of two Hebrew words, namely, machtah, and miqTereth. The former word is generally rendered "censer," sometimes "firepan," and in three cases (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ex 25:38⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:37:23⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:Nu 4:9⟧) "snuffdish" It denoted a bowl-shaped vessel used for different purposes, namely,
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:6 -->
(1) a censer, in which incense was burnt (⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Le 10:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:6 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:8 -->
(2) a firepan, made of bronze, used in connection with the altar of burnt offering (⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ex 27:3⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:8 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:10 -->
(3) a snuffdish, i.e. a receptacle to hold pieces of burnt lamp-wick removed by the tongs or snuffers (⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:Ex 25:38⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:10 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:12 -->
Probably in all these cases the same kind of vessel was meant, namely, a bowl-shaped utensil with a handle, not unlike a saucepan. The other Hebrew word (derived from the same root as the word for "incense") denoted a vessel for conveying incense (⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:Eze 8:11⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:13:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:2Ch 26:19⟧). The Greek word thumiaterion, by which the Septuagint rendered miqTereth, is used also in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:Hebrews 9:4⟧, where the King James Version gives "censer," but the American Standard Revised Version is probably more correct, namely, "altar of incense" (see Commentaries under the word). Compare also ⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Re 8:3⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:16:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:1⟧, where libanotos, properly the adjective of "frankincense," is translated "censer."

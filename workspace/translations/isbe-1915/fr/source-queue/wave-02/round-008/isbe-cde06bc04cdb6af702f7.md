# CAUSE
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
kos: In both the King James Version and the Revised Version (British and American) "for this cause" (the King James Version "cause") occurs in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Ex 9:16⟧ as the rendering of ba`abhur zo'th = "in order that"; "to the end that"; so also in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:Da 2:12⟧ for kol-qebhel denah, and in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:2Ch 32:20⟧ the King James Version for `al-zo'th, where RVS read "because of." In the New Testament the word is used adverbially in the translation of several Greek phrases: heneka toutou (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:Mt 19:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:Mr 10:7⟧); dia touto, ⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:Joh 12:27⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:Ro 1:26⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:9:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:13:6⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:15:9⟧ (the Revised Version (British and American) "therefore"); ⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:1Co 11:30⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:12:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:1Th 2:13⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:13:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:14:2Th 2:11⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:14:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:15:1Ti 1:16⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:15:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Hebrews 9:15⟧; eis touto, ⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:Joh 18:37⟧ (where the King James Version varying the phraseology reads "to this end" "for this cause"); ⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:1Pe 4:6⟧ the King James Version; toutou charin, ⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:Eph 3:14⟧. Unusual renderings occur, as "for his cause" (= "because of"), ⟦ISBE:KEEP:20:2Co 7:12⟧; as = "affair," "thing," obsolete in the King James Version ⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:1Ki 12:15⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:21:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:22:2Ch 10:15⟧, where the word occurs as a paraphrase of necibbah (= "turn of affairs"). In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:23:1Sa 25:31⟧ (King James Version, the Revised Version (British and American)) "causeless" (= without cause the American Standard Revised Version) occurs arbitrarily in adverb sense.

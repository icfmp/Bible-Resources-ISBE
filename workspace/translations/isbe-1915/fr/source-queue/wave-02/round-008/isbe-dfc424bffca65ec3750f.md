# BAHURIM
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
ba-hu'-rim bachurim; (Baoureim usually, but there are variants): A place in the territory of Benjamin which lay on an old road from Jerusalem to Jericho followed by David in his flight from Absalom (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:2Sa 15:32-16:5⟧ ff). It ran over the Mount of Olives and down the slopes to the East. The Talmud identifies it with Ale, math, the modern Almit, about a mile beyond `Anata, going from Jerusalem. If this identification is correct, Wady Farah may be the brook of water (⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:2Sa 17:20⟧). Here Paltiel was parted from his wife Miehal by Abner (⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:2Sa 3:16⟧). It was the home of Shimei, who ran along a ridge of the hill cursing and throwing stones at the fugitive king (⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:2Sa 16:5⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:1Ki 2:8⟧). In Bahurim Jonathan and Ahimaaz, the native messengers of David, were concealed in a well by a loyal woman (⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:2Sa 17:18⟧ ff). Azmaveth, one of David's heroes, was a of Bahurim. In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:2Sa 23:31⟧ we should read, as in ⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:1Ch 11:33⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧

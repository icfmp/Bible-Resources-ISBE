# CHAMBERLAIN
<!-- ISBE-END:1 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:2 -->
cham'-ber-lin: In the Old Testament the word rendered chamberlain, caric, is more properly "eunuch," an officer which oriental monarchs placed over their harems (⟦ISBE:KEEP:3:Es 1:10⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:3:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:4:12⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:4:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:5:15⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:5:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:6:2:3⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:6:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:7:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:7:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:8:21⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:8:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:9:4:4⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:10:f; 6:2⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:10:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:11:14⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:11:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:12:7:9⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:12:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:13:2Ki 23:11⟧). This officer seems also to have had other duties. See under ⟦ISBE:OPEN:14⟧EUNUCH⟦ISBE:CLOSE:14⟧. In the New Testament:
<!-- ISBE-END:2 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:15 -->
(1) oikonomos, literally manager of the household, apparently the "treasurer" as in the Revised Version (British and American) "Erastus the treasurer of the city saluteth you" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:16:Ro 16:23⟧). Compare adapted use as applied to Christian apostles and teachers, bishops, and even to individual members; in which cases, rendered "stewards" (⟦ISBE:KEEP:17:1Co 4:1⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:17:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:18:Tit 1:7⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:18:tail⟧⟦ISBE:KEEP:19:1Pe 4:10⟧⟦ISBE:FIX:19:tail⟧
<!-- ISBE-END:15 -->

<!-- ISBE-BLOCK:20 -->
(2) In ⟦ISBE:KEEP:21:Ac 12:20⟧, "Blastus the king's chamberlain" (ho epi toa koitonos tou basileos, "he who is over the king's bed-chamber"), not treasure-chamber, as above; here praefectus cubiculo, or chief valet de chambre to the royal person, a position involving much honor and intimacy.
